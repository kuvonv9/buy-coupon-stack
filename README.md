## The uncomfortable truth about Best Buy promo codes

Best Buy's own help page on special offers is refreshingly blunt. Promotional codes and digital coupons appear "sometimes." You enter them under Payment Method at checkout. Some are valid in stores only. Digital coupons can't be used toward gift cards. They must be used before their expiration date, in a single transaction, and if a promotion ends at a specific time, the applicable time zone is Central.

Nothing there promises a sitewide discount. That's the part coupon pages don't tell you. What Best Buy does run, regularly:

- **Deal of the Day** and weekly category deals
- **Event pricing** punching hardest around Presidents Day, Memorial Day, back-to-school, and Black Friday
- **Open-box and outlet** inventory, which is where the real percentage lives
- **Trade-in credit** for old phones, tablets, laptops, and consoles
- **Membership perks** on the paid My Best Buy tiers

A coupon code sits at the bottom of that list in terms of typical dollars saved. Aggregator sites also have a structural incentive to list codes that stopped working three weeks ago, because a long list looks more useful than a short accurate one. If a code fails at checkout, that's not you doing it wrong. It's usually just an expired code.

### What the rate actually looks like

This is where honesty matters more than marketing. The Best Buy rate is a campaign rate. It moves.

Tracked historical Best Buy boosts have included 10% back capped at $50 per order, 20% back capped at $50 to $60 per order, and up to 15% back during Black Friday 2025. Those are past promotions, not today's number, and they are not a promise about tomorrow.

The cap is the part people misread. At 10% capped at $50 per order, a $2,000 TV earns $50, not $200. On a $400 pair of headphones, the same 10% hits the cap and pays the full $40. Boosted rates do their best work on mid-priced electronics, not on flagship TVs.

Baseline rates on Best Buy are usually low single digits. The number worth caring about is whatever shows on the Best Buy page the day you're buying, so check it before you click through rather than trusting a screenshot from a deal blog.

### Exclusions are real, so read them first

Boosted Best Buy rates have come with category carve-outs. Commonly excluded: Apple products, graphics cards, game consoles, and high-end appliances, with eligible categories instead covering standard TVs, small kitchen appliances, some computer accessories, audio outside specific brands, action cameras, physical games, and personal care.

Platform-wide, cashback generally doesn't apply to gift cards or store credit, taxes, shipping and service fees, or any portion of an order paid with a gift card or voucher. If your order total is $0 after gift cards, there's nothing to earn on.

## Questions that come up constantly

**Can I combine a Best Buy promo code with cashback?**
Usually yes, if the code is one your cashback platform lists or one Best Buy publishes itself. The order receives the discounted total, and cashback is calculated on what you actually pay. Codes from coupon extensions or other portals are the risky ones.

**Does the Best Buy cashback rate stay the same all year?**
No. It's campaign-based and boosts around sale events. Forget whatever you saw in a screenshot and check the live rate on the Best Buy page before buying.

**Is there a minimum to withdraw?**
US cashout goes to bank transfer or PayPal, and confirmation has to clear first, which follows the retailer's return window. Read your account's current threshold before counting on same-week cash.

**Does it work if I pay with a Best Buy gift card?**
The gift-card portion doesn't earn. Only the amount paid with card or cash does, and a fully gift-card-funded order earns nothing.

**What if the purchase was in store?**
The click-through model can't see in-store transactions. Snaps, the in-app receipt scanner, covers select US retailers with rotating offers, but the Best Buy category is the online one.

**Is a coupon or cashback worth more on a $600 laptop?**
Depends on the day. A live 10% boost capped at $50 pays $50. A working coupon on that order would need to beat $50 at checkout to matter more, and codes of that size are rare outside event windows. Check the rate, then decide.
